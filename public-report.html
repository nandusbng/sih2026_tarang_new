1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & OCEANIC MANDATE

Marine plastic debris, particularly abandoned, lost, or otherwise discarded fishing gear (ALDFG)—commonly known as ghost nets—presents an existential threat to the marine biodiversity of the Indian Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ). These synthetic monofilament and nylon gillnets continue to capture and entangle endangered sea turtles, cetaceans, and benthic organisms indefinitely, disrupting shallow reefs and vital fishing nursery grounds.

To systematically map and eradicate these hazards, the TARANG autonomous hydrographic monitoring fleet was deployed across critical oceanic corridors. Operating dual-frequency Side Scan Sonar (SSS) at 900 kHz in tandem with multispectral satellite monitoring (Sentinel-2 FDI and NDVI indices), the fleet identified high-priority debris clusters spanning both the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal sectors.

2. AUTONOMOUS DETECTION METHODOLOGY

The TARANG mission architecture integrates edge-compute machine learning payloads directly aboard Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs). High-resolution acoustic soundings generate 2D backscatter representations of the seabed, measuring differences in reflectivity and acoustic shadow length. These backscatter swaths are continuously processed by customized deep neural networks, isolating man-made synthetic structures from natural geomorphological formations such as sandwaves, rocky reefs, and benthic outcrops.

Following edge isolation, verified detections undergo multi-tier analyst review before dispatching recovery orders to Remotely Operated Vehicles (ROVs) and coordinated Indian Coast Guard retrieval vessels under the national Sagar Parikrama coastal restoration mandate.

5. RECOVERY ACTION PLAN & PUBLIC CITIZEN ENGAGEMENT

In alignment with India's commitments under the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 14 (Life Below Water), all categorized debris items will be mechanically extracted by recovery catamarans. Recovered ghost nets are transferred to certified coastal recycling centers for thermal conversion into recycled polymer pellets.

Fisherfolk and coastal observers who spot floating debris or unrecovered fishing gear can report observations directly via the TARANG Public Incident Portal, helping autonomous AUV patrols refine their targeted sweep missions.
